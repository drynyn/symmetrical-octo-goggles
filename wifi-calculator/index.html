    Sources and assumptions for the speed estimate
    • Working online — 15 Mbps per person: a peak-use planning allowance for work that may include HD video calls, screen sharing and other concurrent traffic, not a universal requirement. Ofcom broadband guidance and Zoom bandwidth requirements provide context for video-call bandwidth.
    • Gaming — 15 Mbps per person: a planning allowance for online play and related traffic. Gameplay alone often needs less; large downloads and updates are counted separately below.
    • Large downloads or updates — 50 Mbps per active transfer: a provisional peak-use allowance. Actual transfers can use substantially more bandwidth if the connection allows it, so this is not a maximum.
    • HD streaming — 5 Mbps per stream: based on Netflix's recommended internet speeds for 1080p Full HD.
    • 4K streaming — 15 Mbps per stream: based on Netflix's recommended internet speeds for 4K/UHD. Ofcom's guidance may use a more conservative allowance for UHD.
    • Household baseline — 15 Mbps: an illustrative allowance for other concurrent background activity, not an official minimum.
    • Additional active devices — 3 Mbps per device: a planning allowance for devices actively using the connection during peak time. Idle devices generally use far less.
    • Headroom — 30%: an extra margin for variation and overlapping activity; a design choice, not an official standard.

    Published recommendations are context, not guarantees. Actual demand depends on the applications, settings, upload needs and other simultaneous use.

    For this simple estimate, count each person as working or gaming, not both. Count every simultaneous stream separately; a person may watch more than one stream. People not listed under work or gaming are covered by the household baseline. Include large downloads or updates if they are likely to happen during the busiest period.

    Important limitation

    This simple model cannot account properly for wall thickness and angle, insulation, furniture, interference, antenna orientation, client device, channel width, or the hub's band-steering behaviour. It estimates a broad signal range; it does not guarantee speed or reliability. Use measurements from real homes to calibrate it before relying on the result for customer advice.
